# 2026-11-11: test 3, baseline matrix

Rows: `2026-11-11-test3.tsv`. These are Wolf "Test ball" runs: three
codecs (H.264, HEVC, AV1) at three resolutions, 60 fps, 3 runs each, 54
rows. Bitrates were 20, 40 and 80 Mbps for 1080p, 1440p and 4K. No run
came back empty.

| Host (client) | 1080p and 1440p | 4K |
|---|---|---|
| AMD host (NVIDIA client) | 60 in, 59.5 rendered, 0.3-0.4% jitter | HEVC 60 in, 57.5 rendered, 3.6% jitter. H.264 41 in |
| NVIDIA host (AMD client) | 60 in, 57.5 rendered, 1.2-1.7% jitter | about 42.6 in for every codec |

- **AV1 rows are HEVC repeats.** Neither host has a hardware AV1 encoder,
  so every AV1 request was negotiated down to HEVC. AV1 should not have
  been in the matrix; later matrices take codecs from the host's encoder
  probe.
- **4K on the NVIDIA host is capped by the workload, not the encoder.**
  Its Test ball generates frames on the CPU and uploads them to CUDA. That
  holds 42.6 fps for every codec while encoder utilisation stays at
  10-19%. The AMD host's 4K H.264 cap (41 fps) needs the same check. A 4K
  verdict needs a GPU-side source, such as a compositor app.
- **The client path dominates.** On the NVIDIA client, the SDL renderer
  copies on the CPU: render time grows from 0.8 ms at 1080p to 9-12 ms at
  4K, and jitter rises to 3.5%. The AMD client renders 2-3 fewer frames
  than it decodes at every size.
- **Encoder headroom on the NVIDIA host:** 5-19% utilisation throughout.
  The AMD host still has no encoder sampler.
